### R1 — major — Retired contiguous run allocations still bypass the configured maximum size

**Locations:** `crates/krummi-pager/src/scratch.rs:804`, `:818`, `:823`, `:869`; `crates/krummi-tx/src/transaction.rs:697`.

**Verified partial fixes:** Exclusive chunks are now released with their last holder. Recompiled and ran the original deterministic increasing-size reproduction: sixteen writes of runs of 1,025 through 1,040 pages each left `scratch_bytes() == 0` and only 131,072 bytes in `krummi.temp` after transfer. The new `shrinking_runs_under_an_old_read_count_whole_for_the_maximum_size` regression also passed in both storage modes, confirming that a run is counted whole while any of its page entries remains in `versions`.

**Remaining defect and causal evidence:** `State::retire` calls `release(&version)` and then stores the same allocation's `Arc<Slot>` in `retired` (`scratch.rs:823–828`). When the last entry leaves `versions`, `release` removes the holder record and subtracts the entire run's bytes (`scratch.rs:804–819`). However, `free_retired` keeps those allocations until snapshots at or before the retirement sequence bound end (`scratch.rs:869–882`). A still-open reader can therefore retain many complete allocations that `WriteTransaction::memory` no longer charges against the maximum.

This is reachable by extending the already-reported shrinking-reuse scenario with a second reader. The initial reader prevents transfer while overlapping, slightly smaller runs leave surviving tail entries holding their earlier full allocations. Open a newer reader before closing the initial one, and obtain a reference to the current run. Once the initial reader closes, transfer can write every surviving run in commit order (`scratch.rs:625–653`), but retirement retains the complete allocations under the newer reader's sequence bound. Their charge drops to zero while their physical capacity remains allocated. Subsequent replacements obtain budget that is already occupied.

**Executed deterministic reproduction:** Open a new store with a 64 MiB maximum; pause automatic transfer, retain an initial read, and insert runs descending from 1,040 through 1,035 pages. Before each replacement, free the previous run and commit separately so its pages can be reused. Explicit transfer requests remain blocked by the initial read. Open a second read and retain the current run's reference, close the initial read, then transfer. Perform five more replacements, descending from 1,034 through 1,030 pages, keeping the second read open and checking its original bytes after each replacement.

The freshly compiled probe produced the following identical figures in disk and temporary modes:

| Stage | Data bytes | Temporary-area capacity bytes | Counted scratch bytes | Maximum bytes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Six runs, initial reader still open | 8,552,448 | 51,126,272 | 51,011,584 | 67,108,864 |
| Initial reader closed; transferred with second reader open | 8,552,448 | 51,126,272 | 0 | 67,108,864 |
| First further replacement, 1,034 pages | 8,552,448 | 59,596,800 | 8,486,912 | 67,108,864 |
| Fifth further replacement, 1,030 pages | 8,552,448 | 93,396,992 | 42,287,104 | 67,108,864 |
| Second reader closed; transferred | 8,552,448 | 131,072 | 0 | 67,108,864 |

All writes succeeded. Already at the first further replacement, data plus temporary capacity exceeds the maximum. The final temporary capacity alone exceeds it. Disk file lengths matched `area_bytes()` at every stage; temporary mode reproduced the same excess with heap allocations. The held reference remained correct, and ending the second read allowed reclamation. This isolates the failure to retained allocation accounting, not transfer scheduling or journal capacity.

**Public-API confirmation:** Repeated the same two-reader sequence through `krummi::Store`, normal tree `t`, key `b"k"`, with values of `pages * 8192 - 16` bytes. Remove and commit before each insertion. Every insertion, complete-value comparison and held-reference check succeeded. At the end, `krummi.data` occupied 8,568,832 bytes and the files in `krummi.temp` occupied 93,659,136 bytes, against the same 67,108,864-byte maximum. This confirmation uses ordinary supported operations, without internal transfer hooks or file interference.

The verification programs are disposable build/test artifacts under `target/`: the inspected existing `review-large-values-retired-03.rs` was compiled to the new `review-large-values-retired-followup-28.exe`; the public confirmation is `review-large-values-public-retired-followup-28.rs` and its executable. All stores used `tempfile::tempdir()` and were closed after verification.

**Requirement:** The authoritative PRD, `docs/prd/krummi/trees-large-values.prd.md:158–160`, explicitly states:

> En modo temporal y en las versiones que se conservan fuera de `krummi.data`, cuánta memoria reserva el motor de
> más para que un valor fuera de su hoja sea contiguo. Esa memoria cuenta para el tamaño máximo (P10, T14), así que
> cerca del límite un valor puede fallar con tamaño máximo en un modo y caber en el otro con el mismo máximo.

This is R1's existing additional-contiguity-capacity requirement, inherited by the execution's S9 and maximum-size rules. T14 also explicitly counts “en modo temporal, toda la memoria del almacén”.

**Disposition evidence rebutted:** The resolution argues that retired versions are already in `krummi.data`, whose bytes count against the limit, and that retired single pages were already excluded. The latter historical statement is true, but does not justify these retained full-run allocations. The shrinking runs overlap: transfer writes the earlier runs and then overwrites most of each with later runs. Afterward, their superseded bytes do not all remain in `krummi.data`; only their distinct tail pages do. The older complete allocations are retained outside it because of the contiguous representation and conservative snapshot retirement. A data store of approximately 8 MiB cannot account for approximately 49 MiB of these allocations, let alone the subsequent growth. This is the remaining contiguity overhead of original R1, not a new advisory about ordinary single-page retirement.

**Coverage gap:** The added shrinking regression closes its sole retained reader before its final transfer (`crates/krummi-tx/tests/runs.rs:559–567`). It does not leave a second reader open across retirement, so its successful reclamation assertions do not cover this transition.

**Severity and lineage:** **Major**, unchanged: experimentally demonstrated maximum-size failure under supported operations in both storage modes. No data corruption or memory-safety failure is claimed. Preserve the original global identity `docs/reviews/trees-large-values/2026-10-27-code.md#R1`.

**Suggestion:** Keep additional contiguous capacity charged until its allocation is actually released, and cover the two-reader retirement transition.

**Status:** Still open; both earlier scenarios verified fixed, retirement accounting unresolved.
