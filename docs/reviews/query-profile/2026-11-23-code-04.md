### R3 — major — Omitting sparse strata can force a much costlier plan despite correct estimates

- Lineage: [original R3](2026-11-23-code.md#R3), [first follow-up R3](2026-11-23-code-02.md#R3),
  [second follow-up R3](2026-11-23-code-03.md#R3).
- Status: **still open; partially corrected** by `ce885175eea3ed9729695ebb1f88bf2ebe553bc9`, verified at the supplied head.
- Location: `docs/design/tables/15-query-profile-and-tuning.md:494`–496; affected guarantee at lines 509–510.
- Problem: dropping strata containing at most 5% of executions does not bound their share of logical-page cost.
  Renormalizing the remaining weights changes the comparison from the whole workload to its retained subset.
  The resulting confidence rule can therefore force the more expensive plan even when estimates correctly
  separate the expensive values and costs within each stratum have small, well-behaved variance.

**Concrete failure.** Let P be the last good plan and Q the candidate, with these page costs:

| Correctly identified stratum | Workload share | P mean | Q mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A, ordinary values | 99% | 10 | 30 |
| B, rare values | 1% | 100,000 | 100 |

Historical A-only windows with at least 30 executions per plan make Q suspect: its mean 30 exceeds twice P's 10
(lines 470–476). During the trial, both plans remain valid for the catalog, no accepted plan intervenes, and the
pre-draw estimates assign A and B to distinct power-of-two strata. A possible balanced trial gives each arm 99 A
executions and one B execution, reaching exactly 100 per arm. For nonzero variance, P's A costs are
`[9, 11] * 49 + [10]`, and Q's are `[29, 31] * 49 + [30]`.

B has fewer than five executions per arm and holds only 1% of pooled executions. Rule 2 therefore discards it,
renormalizes A's weight to one, and does **not** declare the trial inconclusive. Rule 3 computes:

- Retained means: P = 10, Q = 30.
- Sample variance in each retained arm: 1; variance of each mean: `1/99`.
- `z = (30 - 2*10) / sqrt(1/99 + 4/99) = 44.4971909226`, so the trial forces P.
- Full-workload means: P = `0.99*10 + 0.01*100000 = 1009.9`; Q =
  `0.99*30 + 0.01*100 = 30.7`.

Subsequent traffic with the same mix pays about **32.9 times** Q's cost until directive expiry. This is a
whole-workload regression, rather than merely a worse outcome for a minority of parameter values.

- Evidence type: verifiable technical argument by direct substitution in the new rule, independently executed
  with standard-library Python. A separate seeded simulation with normally distributed noise within the correctly
  separated A stratum also forced P in 5,000 of 5,000 trials; details below. The simulation demonstrates the rule's
  behavior for that stream, not a universal error-rate estimate.
- Violated contract: H30 and R4, which compare and improve a shape's cost, and the approximately 5% normal-within-
  strata protection at lines 509–510. Sparse-stratum exclusion is new in this correction. The documented unbounded
  risk at lines 511–516 concerns estimates that **fail to separate** expensive values; here the estimates separate
  them correctly. The accepted risk does not describe this additional failure. A one-day TTL limits duration but
  does not validate the whole-workload comparison. No runtime data-correctness failure is claimed.
- Suggestion: account for omitted strata's cost and uncertainty before concluding about the whole workload.

## Reproduction details

All calculations ran in memory through `python -B -`; no test file or dependency was created. This complete
arithmetic reproduction exercises the new sparse-stratum rule once it has omitted B as specified:

```python
import math
p_a = [9, 11] * 49 + [10]
q_a = [29, 31] * 49 + [30]
def mean(x):
    return sum(x) / len(x)
def var(x):
    return sum((v - mean(x))**2 for v in x) / (len(x) - 1)
z = (mean(q_a) - 2*mean(p_a)) / math.sqrt(var(q_a)/99 + 4*var(p_a)/99)
print(z)                              # 44.49719092257398
print(.99*mean(p_a) + .01*100000)      # 1009.9
print(.99*mean(q_a) + .01*100)         # 30.7
```

The additional simulation used `random.Random(20261123)`, 5,000 trials, and a fixed parameter stream of two B
executions followed by A executions. Each execution drew its arm with `random() < 0.5`, stopping when both arms
had at least 100 executions. For A, the selected arm then drew cost `10 + gauss(0,1)` for P or
`30 + gauss(0,1)` for Q. Both B executions belong to a correctly identified separate stratum; regardless of
assignment it is omitted (each arm has fewer than five B observations, pooled share at most 1%). Applying the
documented formula to the retained A observations forced P in all 5,000 trials. The largest observed trial had
256 executions. The same underlying 99% A / 1% B mix has expected means 1009.9 and 30.7; the finite trial stream
illustrates sparse observations of that workload.

The normal single-evaluation check used `random.Random(20261124)`, 5,000 trials, one stratum and the same stopping
rule. Each execution first drew `base = 1000 + round(100 * gauss(0,1))`, then its arm with `random() < 0.5`;
P's cost was `base`, Q's `2*base`. Sample variances used denominator `n-1`. There was one evaluation per trial,
with `z > 1.645` in 240 trials. It verifies the changed evaluation policy independently of Claude's claims.
