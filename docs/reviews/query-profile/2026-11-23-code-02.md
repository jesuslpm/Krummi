## Open findings

<a id="R3"></a>
### R3 — major — A short randomized trial can still validate a uniformly worse plan

- Prior finding: [2026-11-23-code.md#R3](2026-11-23-code.md#R3).
- Status: **still open; partially corrected** in `402f125`, verified at the reviewed head.
- Location: `docs/design/tables/15-query-profile-and-tuning.md:480`–484; related claims at lines 855 and 879–881.
- What improved: separate historical windows now trigger suspicion only. Per-execution random assignment
  removes the original systematic association between plan and historical parameter window. With comparable
  realized mixes, the original A/B example clears Q, as the resolution claims.
- Remaining problem: random assignment does not ensure equal realized parameter mixes in a finite trial.
  The specified decision stops at 30 executions per arm and compares only observed means with the factor,
  without an uncertainty check or an inconclusive outcome for insufficient evidence. Exact aggregates of
  those different executions do not establish that the selected plan improves the common workload.
- Concrete scenario, in logical pages:

  | Parameter set | Last good plan P | Candidate Q |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | A | 10 | 1 |
  | B | 10,000 | 1,000 |

  Q is ten times better for every parameter value. Historical P executions are A-only and Q executions
  B-only, with at least 30 each, so Q becomes suspect. The trial then receives one B execution first,
  followed by 59 A executions. A valid random assignment gives P 30 A executions and Q 29 A plus B.
  Both arms reach the threshold at execution 60. P's observed mean is 10; Q's is
  `(29 * 1 + 1,000) / 30 = 34.3`. Since `34.3 > 2 * 10`, the rule declares Q regressed and forces P.
  Subsequent A-only traffic pays ten times Q's cost until the directive expires. Catalog tokens remain
  unchanged, so catalog expiry does not prevent this outcome.
- Evidence type: arithmetic counterexample to the documented decision rule, independently evaluated in an
  in-memory Python check. For the fixed input stream above, just the balanced assignments with B assigned
  to Q have probability `C(59,29) / 2^60 = 0.0512890865` (about 5.13%). None can stop before execution 60
  with 30 per arm. Other assignments need not be counted to establish a material failure probability.
  This does not claim every trial fails or that statistical inference can guarantee zero false positives.
- Violated contract: H30 and R4; the risk mitigation at line 855 claims that forcing selects a plan better
  on average, and experiment 9 says a candidate better for every parameter value must be cleared. The
  fixed-count rule does not support those claims under a realistic skewed workload. The document already
  recognizes that reliable mean comparisons depend on variance (*Sizing T*, lines 416–419).
  No feature-specific S#/acceptance criterion exists for this proposal.
- Suggestion: require uncertainty-aware, comparable evidence before forcing, allow inconclusive trials to
  remain unforced, and state the statistical limits of the guarantee.
